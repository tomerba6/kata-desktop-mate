# M3: Wander, sit, sleep

**Status:** active · **Branch:** `m3-wander` off `main` at `c1b95f9` · **Started:** 2026-11-25

## Context

BRIEF.md's M3: left alone, Kata behaves like a pet. It walks left and right along the floor,
sits, and naps with Zs after a long quiet spell. A click or a grab wakes it. It never leaves the
screen, and its paws don't slide while it walks. A `Brain` state machine decides what Kata does;
the window and the body carry it out.

What shapes the plan, observed in the code:

- **The walk stride is 12 sprite pixels per loop.** In `frames_walk` (`tools/make_sprites.py`), a
  planted paw goes from +3 to −3 (`round(3 - 12 * p)`) over half the loop. At 8 frames and
  10 fps that is 15 sprite pixels per second: 45 Qt units per second at scale 3.
- **M2's window picks the animations itself:** `held`, `fall` and `idle`, in `PetWindow.tick` and
  the mouse handlers. That choice moves into the brain, and the window plays what the brain asks
  for.
- **The window can already be tested headless:** `tests/test_window.py` drives the real
  `PetWindow` on Qt's offscreen platform, with a fake clock and synthetic mouse events.
- **Speed:** one standing `Body.step` takes 0.08 µs (measured). The suite runs 119 tests in
  2.9 s, and the mutation run re-runs it for every mutant, so the new simulation tests must stay
  quick.
- **This machine:** one screen at 150%, with an auto-hiding taskbar, so the floor is the screen's
  bottom edge.

Not in scope: the click hop (M4), the tray menu and the Sit and Sleep menu items (M5), standing on
windows (M7), and slowing the tick while asleep (M8).

## Decisions

From the interview (2026-11-25):

1. **Branch:** `m3-wander` off `main`, merged through a pull request with a merge commit.
2. **The five-minute check:** I launch Kata with KATA_LOG on, and every state change becomes a log
   line. After your five minutes I read the log and check that Kata walked both ways, sat, slept
   and woke, with its feet inside the bounds all along. You judge what a log can't show: paws not
   sliding, the left-facing frames, the Zs. No fast-forward switch.
3. **Napping:** after 90 seconds of quiet, the next time Kata finishes sitting it falls asleep,
   for 30 to 60 seconds. Quiet means no click or grab, and no nap just ended.
4. **Throws turn Kata:** a release with real sideways speed turns Kata to face the way it flies.
   A straight drop keeps the facing it had.

Defaults chosen without asking (approved with the plan):

- **`BehaviourConfig`** in `brain.py`, all times in seconds:
  - each state lasts a random time in its range: idle 2–6, walk 3–8, sit 5–12, sleep 30–60;
  - `sleep_after` 90;
  - `turn_speed` 60 per s: a throw turns Kata only if its sideways speed is at least this, so a
    shaky drop doesn't flip it;
  - the next state is a weighted pick:
    - after idle: walk 3, sit 1, idle 1;
    - after walk: idle 2, sit 1, walk 1;
    - after sit: idle 1, walk 2, or sleep once the quiet spell has lasted `sleep_after`.
- **The brain's interface:**
  - `update(dt, x, walk_range)`, plus the events `grabbed()`, `released(vx)`, `landed()` and
    `clicked()`;
  - it exposes `state`, `animation`, `facing` and `walk_speed`.

  Leftover time carries into the next state, so no state outlasts its range however `dt` is cut.
  A long `dt` gives the same result as many short ones.
- **Events:**
  - `grabbed()` goes to `HELD` from any state;
  - `released(vx)` goes to `FALL`, turning Kata if `vx` is fast enough;
  - `landed()` goes to `IDLE`, and does nothing unless Kata is falling;
  - `clicked()` wakes a sleeping Kata into `IDLE` and does nothing otherwise, until M4;
  - a floor that drops away under a standing Kata sends `released(0)`.

  A click, a grab or a nap ending restarts the quiet spell.
- **Walking:**
  - a new walk heads left or right at random, but away from an edge Kata is standing at;
  - it turns at either end of the walking range and keeps walking;
  - `walk_speed` is the sheet's speed while walking and 0 otherwise.
- **The walking range** is the screen Kata stands on, narrowed so the window stays on the desktop
  (`feet_bounds` of that screen). On two screens, Kata turns at its own screen's edge rather than
  walking onto the other one, whose floor may be higher. `geometry.area_under` finds that screen;
  `floor_under` is rebuilt on top of it.
- **The body walks:** `Body.step` takes a walking speed. A standing body moves sideways by speed ×
  dt, stopping at the bounds; the floor rule is unchanged.
- **Facing left:**
  - every animation has mirrored frames, built once at load with `QTransform().scale(-1, 1)` and
    `FastTransformation`;
  - the mirror is about the window's centre, which is the feet point, so turning never shifts
    Kata sideways;
  - the `held` anchor mirrors with it, to column `frame_w - 1 - x`, so a Kata facing left is
    gripped by its own scruff.
- **The walk stride** is one `STRIDE = 12` constant in `tools/make_sprites.py`, used by the paw
  maths and written as `stride` in the walk entry of `kata.json`. `AnimationInfo.stride` reads
  it, and `sheet.walk_speed(sheet, scale)` works out stride × fps ÷ frames × scale. The redrawn PNG
  must stay pixel-identical (`test_make_sprites.py` checks).
- **Tests of the whole:**
  - The **seeded runs** use the brain and the body on a made-up floor:
    - 200 seeds, ten simulated minutes each;
    - steps between 1/60 and 1/4 s, drawn from the seed;
    - if they take over a second, fewer seeds, measured and logged.
  - The **Hypothesis stateful test** also sends `clicked()` and `released(vx)`.
  - The **smoke test** drives the real `PetWindow` headless, rather than re-wiring `Brain`, `Body`
    and `Animator` by hand as BRIEF words it, so the wiring itself is what's tested. It runs ten
    simulated minutes with the default `BehaviourConfig` and a seeded `random.Random`.
  - **M2's window tests** get a `BehaviourConfig` that keeps Kata idle, so wandering doesn't move
    it under them.
- **Randomness:** the app uses `random.Random()`, seeded by the OS. Tests seed their own.
- **KATA_LOG:** a `state` line on each change, with `from`, `to`, `facing` and `feet`.
- **`load_frames` gets its test:** it was deferred since the dev-tooling plan, and step 4 changes
  `sprites.py` anyway.
- **Mutation runs** after each no-Qt step and again at the close. Every survivor is killed or
  explained in the Session log.

## How to work this plan

- **Resume:** be on the branch above, read this whole file, and take the first step not `[x]`.
  `git log --oneline` on this file shows one commit per finished step; check the ticks against it.
- **One step per request.** For "do 3 and 4", commit each once its checks pass unless it needs
  the user's eyes or ears; the last one waits.
- **Mark the step *(in progress)*** before starting. Wrong branch, or changes that belong to no
  step: stop and ask.
- **Tests first** for logic: see the new test fail for the right reason, then implement.
- **Stop uncommitted:** run *Verify (me)*, mark it *(built, awaiting your ok)*, add a Session log
  line naming any choice this plan didn't settle, and report: what changed, the check and its
  result, what the user should check, what you decided on your own, and "ok to commit it, or tell
  me what to change".
- **The user's ok commits it** ("ok", "approved", or moving on): tick `[x]` with the date, stage
  the step's files and this file by path, and use the step's *Commit* line. Never push.
- **Feedback** on a waiting step: change it, check again, keep it waiting.
- **Plan wrong?** Do the right thing and note it under Deviations; ask first if it changes a
  decision or the scope.
- **Last step committed:** set **Status:** to `done` and add a closing note under the title.
