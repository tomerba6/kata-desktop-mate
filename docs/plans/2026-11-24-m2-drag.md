## Context

BRIEF.md's M2: drag Kata anywhere with the mouse; on release it falls and lands. While carried it
plays `held` with the scruff under the pointer; a press that never moves far enough is a click
(a `clicked` signal for M4). It must not be thrown or dragged off-screen, and idle CPU stays close
to 0%.

What shapes the plan, observed on this machine:

- **M1 left a 60 Hz timer** in `PetWindow` (16 ms, real elapsed time). It grows into M2's main
  tick: step the body, move the window, advance the animator.
- **The grip point:** `held` has 4 frames at 6 fps, with its anchor at (22, 21), the scruff
  behind the head. `fall` has 2 frames at 8 fps.
- **Qt's drag threshold** (`startDragDistance`) read 10 px on the offscreen platform. The app
  reads the real value at runtime.
- **`QtTest` ships with PySide6 6.12.0**, so `PetWindow` can be tested headless.
- **The floor here is the screen's bottom edge** (the taskbar auto-hides), at 150% scaling, on
  one screen.

Not in scope: walking and the brain (M3), the click reaction (M4), standing on windows (M7), and
hand checks on two real screens.

## Decisions

From the interview (2026-11-24):

1. **On release: throw.** Kata keeps the pointer's speed at release, capped. It arcs under
   gravity, stops at the screen edges, and stops sliding when it lands.
2. **Drag checks:** a headless Qt test feeds `PetWindow` fake mouse events in an invisible,
   offscreen test process. Then you drag by hand for feel, with KATA_LOG on, and I read the grab,
   release and landing numbers from the log.
3. **One screen:** the per-screen floor is built from Qt's screen list and tested with made-up
   two-screen layouts. Two real screens stay unchecked, a known gap.
4. **Branch:** `m2-drag`, as CLAUDE.md names it. It reaches `main` through a pull request.

Defaults chosen without asking (approved with the plan):

- **Physics numbers** in a `PhysicsConfig` dataclass in `body.py`, all in Qt units:
  - gravity 2400 per s², so a drop from the top of your 720-high screen lands in about 0.77 s;
  - fall speed capped at 2400 per s;
  - throw speed capped at 1500 per s;
  - release speed measured over the pointer's last 0.08 s.
- **Landing and walls:** no bounce. Landing stops all motion; hitting a side edge stops the
  sideways motion only.
- **Edges:** the window's left, right and top edges stay on the desktop, and the feet never go
  below the floor. At an edge, Kata stops following the pointer and the scruff slips from under
  it.
- **Bounds:** Qt's `availableVirtualGeometry()`, the usable area of all screens as one
  rectangle.
- **Floor:** `floor_under` picks the screen whose columns contain the feet and whose floor is
  the first at or below them. Failing that, the nearest screen.
- **Click or drag:** Qt's own rule. It is a drag once the pointer has moved
  `startDragDistance` or more, counted as the Manhattan distance (sideways plus up/down).
  Otherwise it is a click. Left button only; right-click keeps the Quit menu.
- **A lost release:** if a move arrives with no button down (the release went elsewhere, for
  example after Alt+Tab), it counts as the release.
- **Holding:** the centre of the anchor pixel's scaled block sits under the pointer. During a
  drag the window moves on each mouse event, so it doesn't lag a tick. Otherwise it moves on
  the tick, and only when its rounded position changes.
- **Screens:** queried on each tick, so a taskbar that appears or moves is picked up. step 3's
  CPU measurement confirms this costs little.
- **Time:** `PetWindow` takes a clock function, so the headless test controls time.
  `tests/conftest.py` sets `QT_QPA_PLATFORM=offscreen`, so no test opens a window on your
  screen.
- **KATA_LOG:** new `grab`, `release` (with the speed), `landed` and `click` events, alongside
  M1's `placed`. `PetWindow` is handed a `log` function, which does nothing when the switch is
  off.
- **New no-Qt modules:**
  - `body.py`: feet position, velocity, gravity, landing;
  - `drag.py`: telling a click from a drag, and the release speed.

  Both go into the coverage gate, cosmic-ray's `module-path` and CLAUDE.md's Layout.
  `geometry.py` gains `Bounds`, `grip_feet`, `feet_bounds` and `floor_under`.
- **run-kata measures CPU:** a `--cpu SECONDS` option measures Kata's process time over that
  long. It reports a percentage of one core and of all cores; the second is the figure Task
  Manager shows. So the "idle CPU close to 0%" check is mine, measured before and after M2.
- **Mutation runs** after each no-Qt step and again at the close. Every survivor is killed or
  explained in the Session log (CLAUDE.md, from M2 on).
